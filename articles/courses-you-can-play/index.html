Golf, nothing more

The famous courses you can actually play

November 2026. Most of the courses on your list are open to anyone. The difficulty is not money and it is not membership. It is that each one has its own door, and most people queue at the wrong one.

Three doors, and they work differently

There are really only three ways on to a course like this. A ballot, where you put your name in and wait. A booking window, where the course sells time in advance to whoever is organised, or whoever books the hotel. And a queue, where you turn up early and take your chance.

Pick the wrong one and you end up concluding the place is impossible. It usually is not. Here is how three of the most asked about actually work.

The Old Course, St Andrews

It is a public course. There is no membership to buy and no introduction to arrange. The main route for visitors is the daily ballot.

  • You enter as a group of two to four. Singles cannot enter the ballot and use a separate daily draw, in person, the day before.
  • Entries close at 2pm two days before you want to play, and the results come out after 4.30pm the same day.
  • The course is closed on Sundays, so there is no ballot for that day.
  • You need an official handicap of 36 or better, with a card or certificate from an affiliated club. A letter from a pro does not count.

Your odds are not good on any single day. One estimate, from Ginger Beer Golf Travel, puts visitor success at about one entry in ten across the 2024 season. The Links Trust does not publish its own figures. What that means in practice is that a single attempt is a lottery ticket and a week of attempts is a plan: enter every day you are there and your chances go from slim to better than even.

The ballot green fee ran between £180 and £355 a round in 2026, depending on the season.

If you want a guaranteed round rather than a chance at one, the Swilcan Package is the official route: two rounds on consecutive days, one of them on the Old Course. You apply in a window each autumn and a draw decides the order. For 2027 that window ran from 23 September to 6 October 2026 and has closed, at £745 a golfer in high season and £555 in the shoulder months. The 2028 window is expected around the same time next year.

One date to know if you are planning 2027. The Open returns to St Andrews from 11 to 18 July 2027. The ballot is suspended and the course closes for weeks around it. If your trip is that summer, build it around those dates rather than into them.

Pebble Beach

Also public, and also not quite as simple as it sounds. The green fee was $675 through the end of March 2026 and $695 after that. The question is how you get the time.

To book more than two days out you have to stay at The Lodge, The Inn at Spanish Bay or Casa Palmero, with a minimum of two nights, and three nights for Thursday to Sunday play between September and November. With a minimum room rate around $800 a night, that is roughly $2,275 before you add a cart or a caddie.

The other door is cheaper and less certain. Inside 48 hours anyone can book what is left at the standard green fee. It works best if you are on your own or in a pair, because a gap for four is rare. If it does not come off, Spyglass Hill takes bookings up to three months ahead whether or not you are staying on the property, and nobody comes home disappointed from that one.

Bethpage Black

The opposite model. A state-owned course on Long Island, run by New York State Parks, where the barrier is neither a hotel nor a handicap but a sunrise. Tee times are released online through the state's own system, with residents given a longer booking window than visitors, and the first hour of each day is kept for whoever is physically in the queue. People sleep in the car park for it.

Fees here are a fraction of Pebble, and higher for non-residents than residents. The rules and the numbers have changed more than once, including after the Ryder Cup, so check the state's golf pages before you plan anything around it.

What the three have in common

Nothing, and that is the point. The ballot rewards patience and repetition. The resort rewards money and early planning. The state course rewards getting out of bed. If you treat all three the same way you will miss all three.

And the practical lesson for anyone building a trip: the famous round is the one you cannot control, so book everything else around it. Give yourself several ballot days rather than one. Keep a second course in reserve. Decide in advance what you will do if the answer is no, because for most people on most days the answer is no.

The honest bit

These are figures and rules as published at the time of writing, from the sources below. Prices move every season and booking systems get rewritten, so confirm with the course before you commit to flights.
