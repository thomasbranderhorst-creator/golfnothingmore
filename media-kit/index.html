Who we are

A golf group with a company behind it

The group has been running since 2013 and is one of the largest amateur golf communities anywhere. Since 2026 there is a company behind it, and the reason is simple: a community this size was already being sold to, just not openly and not by us.

We have one rule, and everything else follows from it: members don't sell in here. No affiliate links, no "DM me", no funnels. That rule is what keeps the group worth being in. It is also why a commercial post in here carries weight: the only ones are ours and those of a few partners we let in.

The audience

Who's actually in there

83% of members are men, three in four are 35 or over, and four countries carry three quarters of the group. Here is the detail, including the parts that don't flatter us.

The middle of the working life

The group leans towards the ages where people have both the time and the money for golf. A quarter of members are between 35 and 44.

What the group does on an average day

Member counts are easy to inflate and tell you very little. About 1,400 people post or comment here every day, and an average post picks up 15 comments and 26 reactions. That is the figure we sell against, not the membership number.

Four markets carry three quarters of it

More than half the group is in the United States. Melbourne, Sydney and Perth all sit in the top five cities, so the Australian presence is heavier than the country figure suggests.

01

A standing member deal

Two pages, and you pick

A standing discount code for our members goes on a members-only page they can come back to whenever they want. No deadline, no countdown. Members reach it by subscribing, which is what makes it a member benefit rather than a coupon anyone can find.

If a member-only code is not something you can do, for example because of MAP pricing, your public offers go on our open partner page instead. That one we share freely in the group and it is indexed, so it works harder on reach and less hard on loyalty. Some brands end up on both.

No fee Your standard affiliate terms. You pay on what our members actually buy.
02

Member Deals, our email

A place in Member Deals

Our email to subscribers, once a month. New member codes go out here first and only appear on the members page a week later, so the letter is always ahead of the page. This is also where offers with a limit go: a closing date, a fixed number, early access to something.

Each edition carries one piece of our own data as well: a question we put to the group that month and what they answered. Every email says which links earn us a commission.

Once the list is big enough to be worth it, each edition also carries one sponsored spot: your offer at the top, clearly marked as sponsored.

No fee Commission on sales, agreed in one conversation. A sponsored spot is priced per edition, on request.

Your own posts in the group

Posting under your own name does something a discount code can't: you build a reputation in here. Members see your page come back, get to know it, and remember it. A lot of what that brings in never shows up as a click on the post itself. It comes later, as a follow, a search, a question in your inbox or a recommendation from one member to another.

In return

What we ask of you

A real member offer. Something the public doesn't get: a discount, an extra, early access. A link at the normal price is an advert, and we won't list it.

A standing code, not a three-week promotion. Codes that expire turn our page into a graveyard. If you can only do a limited run, that belongs in Member Deals instead.

Your own code, and monthly numbers. How many orders came through it and what they were worth. We can't see your checkout, and we'd rather ask than guess.

Nothing dressed up as a member's own post. Everything commercial is labelled. That protects your reputation here as much as ours.

Straight answers

What we can't tell you yet

How big the email list is. It started in September 2026. It is small and growing. Write to us and we'll tell you the number that week rather than round it up, and if it's too small to be worth your time, we'll say so.

What a paid post returns. Our first post from our own page, on 18 September, was an announcement with a link in it. It reached 250 people. A few days later we posted a caption contest: a photo, a question and nothing to click. It reached 3,300 people in its first hour, and 14,852 people and 101 comments in three days. Same page, same group. In here, a post that sends people away does not travel, and a post that gives them something to do does. That is also why our first rates are introductory. We can't yet defend them with numbers, and we'd rather say that than dress it up.

Reach is not attention. We've measured posts that reached thousands of people and drew no reaction at all. We sell engagement per post, not impressions.

What our members play and what they spend. Facebook doesn't collect it and we don't keep the answers people give when they join. A member survey is planned. Anyone quoting you a handicap profile for an audience this size is guessing.

One in twenty members is outside the shipping area of most golf brands. When we quote you an addressable audience, we leave those out.
