The 26+ Club: an invitation that worked

A UK golf society for beginners and high handicappers posted one invitation in our group. 3,472 golfers clicked through to apply.

A snapshot from 19 November 2026, three days after the post went up. It is still growing.

The post

"The 26+ Club is open for applications!"

A golf society for beginners, high handicappers, social golfers without an official handicap, and better golfers who just want a laugh and are patient with newer players. Monthly society days at different courses, a buy & sell group, and a trip to Spain in April.

The only requirement is you own a set of clubs, and can take the odd bit of banter.

The comments

Members passed it on

The comments did a lot of the work. Of the first hundred or so:

  • Almost 40 tagged a friendOften with nothing more than a name, or "perfect for you mate" with a laughing emoji. Some of those were meant, plenty were probably a dig: you belong in the high handicap club, mate. Either way, every tag puts the post in front of someone new, and that is a big part of why the reach kept climbing.
  • About 25 asked where they playSurrey, Derbyshire, Scotland, Ireland, North Wales, Essex. Eleven were from the US, Canada and Australia, hoping for something like it there.
  • Golfers checked if they fitLow handicappers asking if they could still join for the fun of it, people without a handicap, players who used to be club members but never found anyone to play with.
  • And some banterJokes about slow rounds and keeping well clear of their tee times. That is part of a golf group, and it keeps a post near the top.
I used to be a member of a club, but I didn't know anyone there so most of the time I played on my own.

Why it worked

Seven things the post got right

  • An invitation, not an ad"Open for applications" and "we'd love to have you". It reads like a club asking you in, not a company selling something.
  • It says who it's forBeginners, high handicappers, golfers without a handicap, and better players with patience. The right members recognise themselves.
  • ConcreteMonthly society days, a buy & sell group, a trip to Spain in April. Nothing vague.
  • One low bar, with a jokeA set of clubs and the odd bit of banter. That is the whole entry requirement.
  • A real photo of a real momentThe organiser at one of their own events, with the club banner behind her. No stock image, no logo on a plain background.
  • One link, at the moment of action"Click here to apply", right after the reader has decided they fit.
  • Easy to pass on"Is this for you?" is a question people answer by tagging a friend. The post spread through the comments.

One thing to add next time: the price. The post says the only requirement is a set of clubs, and the form asks for a membership fee at the end. A few members pointed that out in the comments. Saying it up front avoids that.

What we took from it

Comments spread it, the link turned it into sign-ups

We usually judge posts by comments per 1,000 views, and a strong question post gets around 10. By that measure this one looks weak. But most of its comments were not conversation, they were tags, and each one carried the post further. Then almost 12 link clicks per 1,000 people reached: as much action as a strong question gets in comments. It just happens outside the group, where the sign-up is.

So for an invitation, to join, sign up or try something, one link in the post is fine, and we count the clicks next to the comments.
